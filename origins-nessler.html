The foundational problem: making a wave last

Before Nessler: heated shaping

Marcel Grateau’s heated waving iron offered temporary shaping. It is a relevant technical precursor, not a chemical permanent wave. A temporary wave and a permanent wave must not be treated as the same invention.

The combination of principles

Nessler’s early permanent-wave method combined winding, chemical treatment and heat. The publicly documented London demonstration in October 1906 is a useful chronological anchor. The frequently quoted year 1905 for the machine should be distinguished from this documented demonstration.

The practical engineering challenge

Heat had to be applied while the hair was wound, without damaging the client or operator. Apparatus weight, insulation, treatment time and the placement of heating units became important problems for later designers.

What Nessler set in motion

Nessler did not define one unchanging machine model. The underlying permanent-waving problem opened a market for alternative devices, winding geometries, salon procedures and later chemistry.

There was never just one invention

The permanent wave emerged through different approaches to shaping hair, applying heat, developing chemical treatments and making salon equipment practical. An inventor’s patent, a company’s machine and a widely adopted technique are not the same historical claim.

Marcel Grateau

Heated tongs produced a temporary wave. This important precursor must be distinguished from later lasting, chemically assisted treatments.

Explore the pioneers →

Karl Ludwig (Charles) Nessler

His early permanent-waving process combined winding, chemical preparation and heat. The London demonstration of 1906 is a central milestone.

Nessler and the early process →

Winding methods and Josef Mayer

Different winding geometries posed new challenges for heating devices. Mayer’s US patent 1,619,794 describes a specific appliance arrangement.

Winding and patented apparatus →

Marjorie Stewart Joyner

Her 1928 patent addressed the arrangement and support of multiple heated irons. It solved a different design problem from Mayer’s appliance.

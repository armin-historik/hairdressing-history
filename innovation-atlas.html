Winding: changing the shape of the treatment

Winding is not simply a preliminary step. The geometry of the hair on the rod affects which lengths can be treated and which heating apparatus will fit. Croquignole winding and Mayer’s lateral-opening heater therefore belong together as a question of procedure and equipment, without implying that Mayer invented all end-to-root winding.

Heating: a question of distribution and control

Later inventors and manufacturers explored heating-element designs, temperature application and the handling of electrical equipment. For Suter and Calvete, we must identify the precise apparatus and patent claims before stating that one design was objectively safer, lighter or more efficient.

Apparatus: supporting the work

Joyner’s patent is a clear example of innovation in machine arrangement: the supporting dome and suspension address the practical challenge of positioning multiple heated devices. This is a different problem from Mayer’s flat-winding heater.

Chemistry: a new branch, not a final model

Chemical cold-wave systems changed the relationship between hair treatment and large electrical heating machines. Modern digital perms, in turn, combine chemistry with electronically controlled heat. This is a comparison of principles, not a documented unbroken line of descent.

Methodological rule: A patent is evidence of what was disclosed and claimed; a photograph documents an object or representation; an advertisement documents a commercial claim. None alone proves widespread use or direct influence between inventors.

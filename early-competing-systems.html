Several solutions to a shared challenge

The early permanent-wave industry did not develop through one uninterrupted sequence of machines. Designers, practitioners and manufacturers addressed different problems: how to wind hair, supply and distribute heat, position equipment safely, and make a treatment workable in a salon.

Important distinction: the people and trade names below are research leads. Their presence here does not establish an invention date, a patent priority, a successful product or a direct connection to Nessler.

Gallia: the French apparatus question

Gallia appears in historical accounts as an early French system. Its exact manufacturer, dates, apparatus and claimed advantages must be established from contemporary trade literature or patent records before a detailed comparison can be made.

What would establish the machine?

A contemporary advertisement or catalogue showing the complete apparatus; a dated trade-journal report; a patent identifying its mechanical features; and evidence connecting the trade name to a manufacturer.

Étienne Szeemann: what was actually changed?

The key research question is whether Szeemann’s approach changed the winding procedure, heating arrangement, working method or another aspect of salon practice. Do not present an alleged improvement in temperature control as established without a primary document.

What would establish the improvement?

A drawing or specification demonstrating the winding, heating or salon procedure, plus a contemporary account establishing the claimed change. A personal or professional connection alone does not prove a technical contribution.

Eugène François Suter: heating as a design problem

A patent lead attributes differentiated electrical heating to Eugène François Suter. The original patent specification and drawings must be checked before explaining the switching arrangement in detail. He must not be confused with Marcel J. Suter, who appears in a later Croquignole-related patent.

Which Suter and which patent?

Confirm the full name Eugène François Suter, filing date, jurisdiction, drawings and claims before describing heat regulation. Marcel J. Suter is a different named inventor and must not be conflated with him.

Isidoro Calvete and Icall: invention versus manufacture

Electrical heating equipment associated with Calvete/Icall belongs in the industrial history of permanent waving. It is important to separate a manufacturer’s documented commercial role from an invention personally attributable to Calvete.

Who made it and who invented it?

Look for equipment bearing the Icall name, manufacturer catalogues, patent applicants and inventors, dated advertisements and surviving machine photographs. Manufacturing and patent inventorship are separate forms of evidence.
